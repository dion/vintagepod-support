Privacy Policy for VintagePod (Android)

Last updated: August 6, 2026

In short: VintagePod does not collect, transmit, or sell your personal data. There are no analytics, no ad tracking, and no user accounts. Your music library data and your server credentials stay on your device. The App does look up song metadata — artwork and lyrics — on public music databases, and it does that automatically; the section on Network Connections explains exactly what is sent.

Overview

VintagePod ("the App") is a music player for Android. This policy explains what data the App accesses, how it is used, and where it is stored. The App has no backend server of ours, does not require you to create an account, and contains no advertising or analytics software. There is no Google Analytics, no Firebase, no Crashlytics, and no ad SDK of any kind in the build.

  • No account or sign-up is required to use the App.

Network Connections

The App connects to the network in two distinct ways.

1. Services you choose

When you add a Subsonic-compatible server (Navidrome, Airsonic, and others) or a Jellyfin server, the connection runs directly from your device to the address you entered. We are not a party to it, do not proxy it, and never see it. The same is true of podcast feeds you subscribe to and internet radio stations you play: your device contacts those publishers' servers directly, which necessarily exposes your IP address to them, as visiting any website would.

Streamed music, podcast episodes, and radio are played live. The App does not download them to your device for offline listening.

2. Metadata lookups

To fill in missing cover art and lyrics, the App queries public music databases. These lookups carry only text drawn from your music files' own tags. No account identifier, device identifier, or advertising ID is attached to any of them.

ServiceWhat is sentWhen
MusicBrainz (musicbrainz.org) Album title and artist name Automatically, in a background job after the App scans your library, for each album that has no artwork
Cover Art Archive (coverartarchive.org) A MusicBrainz release identifier only — no text of yours Same background job, when MusicBrainz returned a match
Apple's iTunes Search API (itunes.apple.com) Artist name and album title Same background job, as a fallback when MusicBrainz has no artwork
LRCLIB (lrclib.net) Song title, artist name, album title, and track duration Automatically when a track starts playing, if lyrics for it are not already stored on the device and the App is unlocked for lyrics. Each song is looked up once
radio-browser.info The search text you type, or the country or genre you tap Only when you browse or search for radio stations

Artwork images returned by these lookups are downloaded from the hosting service (for iTunes results, Apple's artwork CDN). Each of these services operates independently of us and applies its own privacy policy and its own server logging to the requests it receives.

These lookups currently have no in-app off switch. The artwork job runs after each library scan and the lyrics lookup runs on track change; if you do not want them to happen, the way to prevent them is to deny the App network access at the operating-system level.

Server Credentials

Server addresses, usernames, and passwords or API keys are stored as plain text in the App's private preference storage. That storage is protected by the Android application sandbox — other apps cannot read it — but the App does not apply its own encryption on top, and we do not claim that it does. Because the App permits Android's standard backup mechanism, these files may be included in device backups to your Google account if you have Android backup switched on.

How the credential is used depends on the server type:

  • Subsonic-compatible servers: your password is never sent to the server. Each request carries a salted MD5 hash of it, as the Subsonic API specifies. Note that these parameters travel in the request URL, so they may appear in your own server's access logs, and the App uses whatever address you enter — if that address is plain http://, the traffic is not encrypted in transit.
  • Jellyfin servers: the App signs in by sending your username and password to your server, which is how the Jellyfin API works. The access token it returns is held in memory only and is not written to disk, so the sign-in happens again the next time the App starts. If you supply an API key instead of a username and password, that key is sent as a request parameter.

Purchases

Purchases inside the App are processed by Google Play Billing. We never see or handle your name, address, or payment details — Google does not give them to us. The App only ever learns whether the Google account signed in on the device owns each of its products, and when a purchase was made. Payment data is governed by Google's Privacy Policy and the Google Play Terms of Service.

Data Sharing

VintagePod does not share, sell, rent, or disclose your data to any third party. Beyond the metadata lookups described above — which carry song titles and artist and album names only — nothing about you or your library leaves your device on our behalf.

Children's Privacy

VintagePod does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13. Since the App stores everything locally on-device and has no account system and no data collection, there is no personal data for us to hold about any user.

Your Choices

  • You can revoke the App's access to your audio files at any time in Settings > Apps > VintagePod > Permissions.
  • You can remove a configured streaming server from within the App, which deletes its saved address, username, and password from your device.
  • Clearing the App's storage, or uninstalling it, removes the library database, your settings, your ratings and playlists, cached artwork, and cached lyrics from your device.
  • Purchases are recorded against your Google account rather than your device, so they are not lost by clearing storage or uninstalling, and can be restored from the App's Pro screen.
