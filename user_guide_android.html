VintagePod · User guide · Android

Everything runs through the wheel.

VintagePod is a click-wheel music player for Android. There is no bottom bar and no swipe navigation — you spin, press, and hold a single wheel to reach every screen. Learn the wheel and you know the whole app.

Android 8.0 or later

Pick a zone to read what it does

The ring

Spin to move

Drag your finger around the outer ring. Every 15 degrees clicks one step — down the list, along Cover Flow, up the volume. Each click gives a short buzz and a tick, so you can feel your place without looking.

Good to know: the four labels are printed on the ring, but the zones are wedges — the whole top quarter is MENU, the whole bottom quarter is play/pause. You never have to aim.

Reference

Every gesture

Six inputs cover the entire app. Screens change what a spin means — never what a press means. Going back is MENU, not the Android back gesture — the wheel is the only navigation.

First run

Your library finds itself

The first time you open VintagePod it asks for permission to read audio on your device. Allow it, and the library builds itself in the background — no import step, no folder picking. Tags, artwork, ratings and genres are read straight from your files.

Supported formats: mp3 · flac · aac · m4a · ogg · wav. High-resolution files keep their full sample rate and bit depth, and are badged as such on Now Playing.

If you said no by mistake

Nothing is lost — the menus still work, they are just empty. Grant the permission in Android's app settings (Permissions › Music and audio) and reopen VintagePod. The scan starts on its own.

Adding music later

Copy files into your device however you normally would — a cable, a cloud app, a download. VintagePod picks up changes in the background, so new albums appear without you doing anything. Artwork comes from the file's own tags or from a cover.jpg beside it; missing covers are looked up online when the tags identify the album well enough.

Browsing

Music

Main › Music

The Music menu is the whole library, cut six ways. Any configured streaming server appears at the bottom of the same list, so local and remote browsing feel identical.

  • Cover Flow — spin through album art. Select opens the album; ▶❙❙ plays it where it stands. Hidden when Monochrome is set to Authentic, which predates album art.
  • Search… — type once, get artists, albums and songs in one list. Song results play as a queue, so the search itself becomes a playlist.
  • Playlists — your playlists, plus On-The-Go, plus a rating shelf: ★★★★★, ★★★★ & Up, and so on down to one star.
  • Artists · Albums · Songs · Genres — the ordinary way down. Albums show their year, songs show their length.
  • Edit Genre — hold centre inside an album to retag every song on it at once.

On-The-Go

Hold the centre button on any song, album, artist or genre and choose Add to On-The-Go to queue it up — the whole album if you held an album row. Hold a song inside a playlist to remove it again. On-The-Go lives with your other playlists and survives restarts.

The player

Now Playing has five faces

Main › Now Playing

Press the centre button to cycle through the stages. Whatever stage you are on decides what spinning the wheel does — that one rule explains the whole screen.

  1. ArtworkThe default. Spin to change the device volume; a level meter appears while you turn and fades a moment after you stop.
  2. ScrubSpin to move through the track. Stops listening after three still seconds and drops back to artwork on its own.
  3. LyricsSpin to scroll. VintagePod checks its own cache, then the lyrics embedded in the file, then looks online — synced lyrics win when both exist.
  4. RatingSpin to set nought to five stars. Ratings feed the rating shelf in Playlists.
  5. GenreSpin to reassign the song's genre from the genres already in your library.

What else is on screen

  • Track counter — position in the current queue, like 4 of 12.
  • HI-RES — shown when the file is better than 48 kHz or deeper than 16-bit.
  • Hold centre — options for the playing track: add it to On-The-Go, or jump to its album or artist.
  • Outside the app — playback runs as a media service, so the notification, the lock screen, headset buttons, Bluetooth controls and your car all see the track and can control it. Playback continues when you leave VintagePod.

Listening out

Radio and podcasts

Main › Radio  ·  Main › Podcasts

Radio

Browse a public directory of internet stations by Country or by Tag, or type a name into Search…. Each result shows how many stations sit behind it. Select a station to open its options: Play, or Add to Favorites so it is waiting at the top of the Radio menu next time.

Podcasts

Choose Add Podcast… and paste an RSS feed URL. VintagePod reads the feed and lists the episodes, newest first, with dates and lengths. Select an episode to stream it. Unsubscribe sits at the bottom of the show's own episode list.

Your own library, elsewhere

Servers

Settings › Servers › Add Server…

VintagePod streams from Subsonic-compatible servers and from Jellyfin. Fill in a name, the server URL, and either a username and password or — for Jellyfin — leave the username blank and give an API key. Press Test to check the connection before you Save.

Saved servers appear at the bottom of the Music menu. Browsing goes artists → albums → songs, exactly like the local library, and playback and Now Playing behave the same way. Credentials are kept in VintagePod's private app storage, and Subsonic passwords are sent as a salted token rather than in the clear. Select a server row in Settings to reopen its details, change them, or Delete it.

Making it yours

Settings

Main › Settings

Appearance

Each row cycles its value when you press Select — there are no sliders or switches to hunt for.

  • Body Color — Graphite, Silver, White, Red, Blue.
  • Wheel Theme — Light or Dark wheel against that body.
  • Dark Mode — System, Light, or Dark for the screen itself.
  • Ultra Dark — dims the screen further for night listening.
  • Monochrome — Smooth renders a modern greyscale screen; Authentic reproduces the coarse early-model panel, pixel spacing and all. Authentic mode fixes its own text size.
  • Text Size — Small, Default, Large, plus Reset Text Size. Hidden in Authentic mode.
  • Immersive Mode — hides the Android system bars and keeps the screen awake, iPod-in-a-dock style. On by default.
  • Glossy Finish, Gloss Angle, Gloss Position — the sheen across the body: on or off, shallow through steep, left to right.

Equalizer

Twenty-two presets, from Acoustic to Vocal Booster, plus Custom. Custom exposes as many bands as your device's own equalizer offers — usually five. Spin to pick a band, press Select to grab it, spin to move it, press again to let go. Start playing something first: the custom EQ needs a live audio path to attach to.

The rest

  • Clicker — the wheel's tick sound, on or off. The haptic buzz stays either way.
  • Language — System, plus English, Español, Français, Italiano, 日本語, Português and 中文.
  • Date & Time — Time in Title puts a clock in the title bar of every screen.
  • Repeat — Off, All, or One.
  • About — version and credits.

If something looks wrong

Quick fixes

  • The library is empty. The audio permission was declined, or the scan hasn't finished. Check Permissions › Music and audio in Android's settings for VintagePod, then reopen the app.
  • Some albums are missing. Files in formats VintagePod can't decode are skipped. Everything in the supported list above will appear once the background scan reaches it.
  • No lyrics. The screen says so once the search finishes. Nothing found is remembered, so the same track won't hit the network again.
  • Text is too small to read. Appearance › Text Size, unless Monochrome is set to Authentic — that mode sets its own size on purpose. Switch it to Smooth to get the control back.
  • The wheel feels imprecise. Spin in wide circles near the outer edge. Small movements near the centre count as a press, not a spin.
  • The system bars are gone. That is Immersive Mode. Turn it off in Settings › Appearance.
